Tidal stream energy offers a predictable, dispatchable domestic resource with theoretical US potential exceeding 400 TWh/year. Arraying turbines across tidal channels introduces flow confinement that amplifies power extraction and turbine loading beyond open-flow operation, while creating multi-scale coupling between blade-level flow physics and array-scale mixing. Existing analytical models of tidal array performance (Garrett & Cummins 2007; Nishino & Willden 2013) require closure parameters — confinement-corrected induction fields and near-wake turbulent mixing rates — that actuator disk theory cannot provide, and no blade-resolved high-fidelity simulations of confined tidal arrays currently exist to supply them. 

This proposal develops a three-aim multi-fidelity modeling hierarchy to close this gap. Aim 1 uses the NLR ExaWind stack to conduct blade-resolved hybrid RANS/LES simulations of axial-flow tidal turbines in confined channels. These simulations span a lone turbine to a full cross-stream array, and aim to quantify confinement-driven hydrodynamic mechanisms and deliver physics-informed corrections to actuator disk and line methods and to OpenFAST. Aim 2 implements these corrected rotor models in OpenFOAM to investigate free-surface effects across Froude number and submersion depth regimes, evaluating the validity of rigid-lid assumptions at shallow submersion and high array packing densities. Aim 3 distills the physical insights from Aims 1 and 2 into an open-source tidal array engineering design framework and applies it to site-specific power extraction assessments at Cook Inlet, the Gulf of Maine, and Puget Sound.All simulation tools and datasets will be released open-source, with model corrections submitted for peer-reviewed publication for broad community adoption.
